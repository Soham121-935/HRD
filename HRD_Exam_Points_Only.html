UNIT 1 — Q1–20

Q1. Concept of HRD and major objectives

  1. Def: planned continuous process to grow KSA + potential + culture (not one training day).
  2. Nadler 1969: organised learning, fixed time, aimed at behavioural change.
  3. T.V. Rao: (i) present & future role capabilities (ii) inner potential (iii) teamwork culture.
  4. Obj: sharpen present/future job skills.
  5. Obj: equity, employability, adaptability.
  6. Obj: develop individual, dyad (boss–subordinate), team, whole organisation.
  7. Obj: enabling climate (initiative, experiment, renew).
  8. Tools: appraisal, training, counselling, OD — check they help, not just paperwork.

Q2. Evolution: training → strategic HRD

  1. Stage 1: welfare/personnel — files, discipline, wages; little development.
  2. Stage 2: training cell — short job-skill courses, isolated from strategy.
  3. Nadler 1969 names HRD as a field (training + education + development).
  4. India 70s–80s: Rao/Pareek — HRD as a SYSTEM (appraisal, potential, career, OD).
  5. HRM = maintenance; HRD = development; every manager responsible.
  6. Liberalisation/IT/quality → need capability for competition = strategic HRD.
  7. Today: competencies, talent pipeline, climate, e-learning, seat at strategy table.
  8. Exam line: isolated workshop = old stage; linked to goals = strategic.

Q3. Relationship HRD and HRM

  1. HRM = umbrella (hire, pay, law, welfare, develop, use people).
  2. HRD = development part inside HRM (learning, potential, climate).
  3. HRM maintenance-oriented; HRD development-oriented.
  4. HRM often money-motivation; HRD higher-order needs (growth, recognition).
  5. HRM dept’s job vs HRD = ALL managers’ job.
  6. They need each other: fair pay (HRM) so people can learn (HRD).
  7. Data link: appraisal files → training/succession.
  8. Not same word, not unrelated — complementary.

Q4. Importance of HRD for performance

  1. Skill: close gap (standard − actual) → fewer errors, more output.
  2. Role clarity + feedback → effort not wasted.
  3. Motivation/morale → extra effort, less turnover.
  4. Use potential (right person in right future role).
  5. Teamwork/dyads → organisation is a system, not 1+1 people.
  6. Adapt + innovate in changing environment.
  7. Cut waste: accidents, absence, attrition costs.
  8. Long-term effectiveness, not fear-driven one-month spike.

Q5. HRD climate + supportive characteristics

  1. Climate = how it FEELS now regarding trust, learning, fairness (not posters).
  2. OCTAPAC: Openness, Confrontation, Trust, Autonomy, Proactivity, Authenticity, Collaboration.
  3. Openness: ask doubts, 2-way talk.
  4. Trust/authenticity: words = actions; no mask.
  5. Confrontation: solve problems, don’t hide.
  6. Autonomy + initiative; collaboration across depts.
  7. Top support: time, money, release for training.
  8. Fair access + recognise mentoring. Soil for all HRD tools.

Q6. HRD climate vs organisational culture

  1. Culture = deep values/beliefs/rituals (‘who we are’) — slow to change.
  2. HRD climate = current feel of development/trust — can change faster.
  3. Culture = broad (power, dress, stories); climate = learning & feedback only.
  4. Eg culture: family firm, seniority, owner-as-father.
  5. Eg climate in same firm: seniors teach vs hide knowledge.
  6. Mismatch: ‘learning culture’ on wall + punitive appraisal = cynicism.
  7. HRD must FIT culture and IMPROVE climate.
  8. Exam: culture = identity; climate = today’s weather for growth.

Q7. Role of HRD professional

  1. Not a clerk who books a hall.
  2. Diagnose needs (TNA, climate, strategy) — say when training is NOT the answer.
  3. Design integrated system (appraisal–career–training–counselling).
  4. Facilitate learning (trainers, e-learning, mentoring) + train bosses to coach.
  5. Climate/culture builder + change agent (merger, IT, quality).
  6. Evaluate/audit: did behaviour change?
  7. Ethics: confidentiality, fairness.
  8. Shift: administrator → business partner.

Q8. Significance for effectiveness + long-term development

  1. Effectiveness = goals + adapt + healthy inside — done BY people.
  2. Competence engine for quality/service.
  3. Self-renewal even if growth has stopped (environment still changes).
  4. Align IDPs to organisational goals — no random courses.
  5. Leadership pipeline / succession.
  6. Growth beyond present job → retention.
  7. Humanistic + economic (people as assets with unlimited potential).
  8. Cut HRD → short profit, long decline.

Q9. Growing firm: skill gaps + falling performance (apply HRD)

  1. Diagnose first: skill vs machine vs overload vs unclear goals.
  2. Fix climate (fear hides gaps).
  3. Developmental appraisal + counselling, not only punishment.
  4. Targeted mix: OJT + short courses from TNA (no generic ‘motivation’ camp).
  5. Train newly promoted bosses to coach (hidden cause).
  6. Career/potential for NEW roles created by growth.
  7. Urgent quality/safety gaps first, then yearly HRD cycle.
  8. Evaluate errors/productivity; repeat diagnosis.

Q10. Measures for learning-oriented environment (apply climate)

  1. Leaders model learning and admit mistakes.
  2. Psychological safety: report errors without humiliation.
  3. Frequent coaching feedback, not only annual rating.
  4. Time + budget + permission to practise on the job.
  5. Team forums: after-action review, quality circles, mentors.
  6. Fair access (not only favourites).
  7. Reward knowledge-sharing, not only individual heroes.
  8. Align appraisal/promotion with learning & teamwork.

Q11. Any two objectives of HRD (expand to 8 marks)

  1. Obj 1: acquire/sharpen capabilities for PRESENT and FUTURE roles + example.
  2. How: induction, training, coaching, rotation, appraisal gaps.
  3. Obj 2: develop general capabilities + inner potential (whole person).
  4. How: potential appraisal, career, mentoring, education.
  5. Also culture of teamwork (Rao’s 3rd pillar) — write 8 lines.
  6. Also equity, employability, adaptability.
  7. Also 7 system goals: individual, role, future, dyad, team, collaboration, self-renewal.
  8. Don’t stop at 2 sentences — explain + example each.

Q12. Any two ways HRD & HRM are related

  1. Relation 1: HRD is the development CORE inside HRM umbrella.
  2. Eg: hire (HRM) then induct/train/career (HRD).
  3. Relation 2: complementary — maintenance vs growth need each other.
  4. Shared data (appraisal → training), shared line managers, shared strategy.
  5. Then 5-line DIFFERENCE so you don’t mix terms.
  6. HRM money vs HRD growth-needs; HR dept vs all managers.
  7. Strategic HRM is delivered THROUGH HRD.
  8. Not identical, not unrelated.

Q13. Weak HRD climate → behaviour & performance (analyse)

  1. Chain: weak climate → behaviour → results.
  2. Fear → hide mistakes → late crises/accidents.
  3. No initiative → wait for orders → slow in market.
  4. Training won’t transfer (‘forget classroom’).
  5. Political appraisal → fake data, no improvement.
  6. Silos/blame → customer delay.
  7. Demotivation, absence, exit of good people.
  8. Unused potential → growth plans fail. Climate = performance issue.

Q14. Changing role of HRD professional (analyse)

  1. From record-keeper → diagnostician.
  2. From hall-organiser → learning architect (blend methods).
  3. From isolated training cell → integrator of subsystems.
  4. From edge → strategy table (business literacy).
  5. Change agent (merger, digital, quality).
  6. Developer of LINE managers as coaches.
  7. From counting programmes → evaluation + ethics.
  8. Why: competition + human capital. Title change ≠ role change.

Q15. Philosophy, climate, performance (relationship)

  1. Philosophy = leaders’ belief: people have potential, development is duty.
  2. Philosophy → practices (time, coaching, fair appraisal).
  3. Practices → climate (what employees FEEL).
  4. Climate → behaviour (initiative vs fear).
  5. Behaviour → performance (quality, service, innovation).
  6. Reverse: good results strengthen philosophy; panic cuts HRD → downward spiral.
  7. Worst: posters ‘people first’ + cruel bosses = cynicism.
  8. Manage as ONE chain, not 3 slogans.

Q16. Evaluate: HRD strategic vs only training

  1. Training = short job-skill learning — necessary TOOL.
  2. Training-only = calendar, smile sheets, cancelled when busy.
  3. For strategic: capability, pipeline, culture execute strategy.
  4. Training-only fails transfer if appraisal/career ignore it.
  5. Limit: small firm may need only basic training; ‘strategic’ without data = fashion.
  6. Verdict: YES strategic function; training nested inside it.
  7. Implies: seat in strategy, line KRAs, evaluate behaviour/results.
  8. Don’t insult trainers; don’t equate calendar with HRD.

Q17. Any two indicators of supportive HRD climate

  1. Ind 1: openness + free useful 2-way feedback (juniors speak; mistakes discussed to learn).
  2. How see: survey item, meetings, not waiting 1 year for news.
  3. Ind 2: visible management support (time, budget, release for training, leaders learn too).
  4. How see: actual training days, IDPs done, distant branches included.
  5. Also: trust, autonomy, collaboration, fairness, recognition of mentors.
  6. Teamwork without blame-notes.
  7. Indicators = observable signs, not slogans.
  8. Name 2 in depth + 3 extra = 8 marks.

Q18. Two reasons HRD pro must understand culture

  1. Reason 1: FIT of tools — 360°/open feedback may fail in hierarchical culture (fake data).
  2. Eg: start with private coaching, then widen.
  3. Reason 2: pace change — read climate scores against culture; don’t shock overnight.
  4. Also: who must sponsor (owner/union/CEO) for legitimacy.
  5. Subcultures (plant vs IT vs region) — one design won’t fit all.
  6. Strategy needs a culture (innovation vs cost) → HRD agenda.
  7. Ethics: don’t crush people’s meaning of work.
  8. Culture-blind HRD = neat and rejected.

Q19. Evaluate: HRD without culture/climate

  1. Fake data / silent classrooms.
  2. Ritual forms, no behaviour change.
  3. Cynicism — worse trust than doing nothing.
  4. Wasted money; no transfer (levels 3–4 fail).
  5. Value clash → employee stress.
  6. Opportunities captured by in-groups (inequity).
  7. Only ‘positive’: glossy optics for visitors — reject as true benefit.
  8. Verdict: diagnose → adapt → implement → evaluate. Blind copy = unprofessional.

Q20. Design basic HRD framework (capability + goals)

  1. 1 Policy: every manager develops people; HRD facilitates; equity.
  2. 2 Translate goals → role competencies (alignment hinge).
  3. 3 Subsystems linked: appraisal, potential, training, career, feedback, counselling.
  4. 4 Climate (OCTAPAC, time to practise).
  5. 5 TNA (org/task/person; non-training causes).
  6. 6 Mix: OJT, courses, mentoring; IDPs; succession.
  7. 7 Governance: business heads review capability vs strategy 2×/year.
  8. 8 Evaluate + HRD audit; redesign. Closed loop, not random courses.
UNIT 2 — Q21–40

Q21. Structure of HRD system + components

  1. System = parts working for one purpose; HRD is PROCESS not bag of forms.
  2. Base: philosophy + 7 goals (individual→self-renewal).
  3. Components: performance appraisal, potential, training, career.
  4. Feedback + counselling (the human conversation).
  5. OD / climate (teams & culture).
  6. Supports: line managers, HRD cell, records, HRM links (pay/promotion).
  7. Structure = LINKAGE: cycle of data from one to next.
  8. Review: do mechanisms help or hinder development?

Q22. Performance appraisal as HRD subsystem + importance

  1. HRD appraisal = goals → review → 2-way feedback → IDP (not secret increment mark).
  2. Importance: role clarity.
  3. Diagnose strengths/gaps → raw material for HRD.
  4. Institutionalises feedback.
  5. IDP = heart (else only judgement).
  6. Feeds training, career, potential, counselling.
  7. Motivates + develops boss–subordinate dyad.
  8. Fails if forced ranking, no talk, no follow-up — then it HARMS development.

Q23. Training as HRD subsystem + contribution

  1. Def: planned effort to learn job KSAs (Noe). Need ≈ standard − actual IF cause is skill.
  2. Place: receives TNA/appraisal; must transfer back to job.
  3. Contributes knowledge + skill + attitude (not lecture-only).
  4. Present-job effectiveness + future roles/change.
  5. Morale, employability, lower turnover (notes’ benefits).
  6. Induction = socialisation into culture.
  7. Limit: cannot fix machines/cruel bosses; not whole HRD.
  8. Maximise: TNA, right method, boss support, evaluate.

Q24. Career planning + potential appraisal

  1. Potential = can they handle BIGGER/different job? Separate from this year’s rating.
  2. Avoid Peter Principle (promote till they fail).
  3. Career planning = choose goals + path; blueprint; continuous (env changes).
  4. Individual’s job + org must counsel & offer chances; integrate goals.
  5. Process: aspirations → opportunities → match/mismatch → strategies → review.
  6. Link: potential makes paths real; career turns labels into rotation/training.
  7. Benefits: succession, retain talent, morale.
  8. Caution: secret lists, bias, only one ladder (allow specialist path).

Q25. Role of feedback and counselling

  1. Feedback = specific, timely, behaviour-based info (not ‘you are poor’).
  2. Two-way: employee also reports obstacles.
  3. Counselling = helping talk on emotional/work problem so PERSON decides (notes).
  4. Functions: advise, reassure, communicate, release tension, clarify, reorient.
  5. Aims: self-understanding, achievable goals, coping, positive self-regard.
  6. Links appraisal/training/career to a living person.
  7. Who: manager (trained) or specialist; confidentiality; refer serious cases.
  8. Process: describe behaviour → listen → agree solution → commit → follow up.

Q26. Principles for designing effective HRD practices

  1. Top commitment + lived philosophy.
  2. Line-manager ownership (every boss a developer).
  3. Integrate subsystems (no showcase course in a silo).
  4. Align with strategy (right capabilities).
  5. Need-based diagnosis (no off-the-shelf).
  6. Fit culture; build climate slowly.
  7. Participation, fairness, transparency (equity).
  8. Continuity + evaluation (help or hinder the process?).

Q27. How HRD subsystems are interrelated

  1. Appraisal → counselling agenda + training need.
  2. Training → next appraisal checks transfer.
  3. Potential → career path (leader vs specialist).
  4. Career plan → which course + which rotation.
  5. Feedback = blood of the system (every stage needs talk).
  6. Climate/OD = soil; fear = fake data everywhere.
  7. HRM consistency: if bonus kills teamwork, training of teamwork dies.
  8. One competency file so the year doesn’t start from zero.

Q28. Need to integrate HRD with strategy

  1. People execute strategy (digital/quality/expansion fail without skill).
  2. Filter waste: don’t train fashion topics.
  3. Talent TIMING: 20 team leaders in 2 years must start NOW.
  4. Climate type must match strategy (cost vs innovation).
  5. Change support (counselling, reskill) or circulars stay on paper.
  6. HRD credibility/budget depends on business link.
  7. Employees learn better when they know WHY.
  8. Practice: competency maps, IDPs in business reviews, drop obsolete courses.

Q29. Appraisal exists but doesn’t help development — improve

  1. State purpose = development (not only increment).
  2. Joint goals at START of year.
  3. Train bosses: incidents, not labels; listen.
  4. Protect counselling time; no corridor signatures.
  5. End with IDP + quarterly follow-up.
  6. Link gaps to TNA/career/potential.
  7. Climate: no public humiliation; some process appeal.
  8. Survey ‘did this help you grow?’ — judge managers on talk quality, not form date.

Q30. Development path for high leadership potential

  1. Validate potential (multi-rater / assessment centre); tell the person honestly.
  2. Career map 3–5 yrs + their aspirations; more than one fork.
  3. Keep current job performance strong + leadership behaviours on appraisal.
  4. STRETCH: rotation, project lead, deputy — main developer.
  5. Training: business concepts + people skills, timed with stretch.
  6. Mentor + counselling (pressure, arrogance derailers).
  7. Rich feedback (360 if climate allows).
  8. Yearly review; if wrong label, specialist path is also honourable.

Q31. Any two HRD subsystems

  1. 1 Performance appraisal — developmental review + IDP + example.
  2. Why ‘sub’: diagnoses, doesn’t itself create skill.
  3. 2 Training — planned KSA learning; TNA-based + example.
  4. Why ‘sub’: not whole HRD; needs climate/career.
  5. Also name: potential, career, counselling, OD.
  6. One link sentence: gap → counsel → train → career → re-appraise.
  7. Don’t write two words only.
  8. Intro + 2 long paras + others = 8 marks.

Q32. Any two purposes of feedback

  1. P1: improve CURRENT performance (same-day, specific) + example.
  2. Tone: respect so person stays open.
  3. P2: long-term development (reinforce strengths; guide training/career).
  4. Also motivation (recognition).
  5. Also role clarity (dyad).
  6. Also climate (openness/trust signal).
  7. Also upward feedback + training evaluation.
  8. Not scolding, not vague praise.

Q33. Poor coordination among subsystems (analyse)

  1. Rated weak every year, never trained → gap stays, trust dies.
  2. Courses with no chance to use / no career → picnic.
  3. High potential ignored in promotion → talent exits; HRD looks fake.
  4. No counselling after rating → form without growth.
  5. Conflict: train teamwork, bonus only individual → people follow pay.
  6. Duplication + some roles get nothing; no memory file.
  7. Chaos → worse climate → even less transfer.
  8. Vicious cycle: no impact → budget cut → worse HRD.

Q34. Align HRD with strategy in RAPID change

  1. Every strategy shift → update competency list FAST.
  2. Short TNA/climate pulses, not 3-year studies.
  3. Flexible methods: JIT, OJT, e-learning, short workshops.
  4. OD/counselling for mergers/new teams (not only skill).
  5. Career: projects & lateral moves, review often.
  6. HRD in the business meeting every quarter.
  7. STOP obsolete courses/KRAs (alignment = subtraction too).
  8. Measure speed of capability, not number of programmes.

Q35. Evaluate potential + performance appraisal together

  1. Different questions: how well NOW vs how far FUTURE.
  2. Avoids wrong promotion of star specialist to bad manager.
  3. 4 boxes: high/high leader track; high perf/low pot specialist; etc.
  4. Fairer if transparent.
  5. Risks: secret lists, bias, 5-minute dual rating, no action.
  6. Conditions: separate tools, trained assessors, feedback, follow-up moves.
  7. Either alone is incomplete.
  8. Verdict: together YES if quality; paperwork combo NO.

Q36. HRD designed without strategy (analyse ineffectiveness)

  1. Develop yesterday’s skills; strategy needs new ones.
  2. Budget on popular courses; critical roles stay weak.
  3. Career promises that don’t match future structure → anger.
  4. Appraisal chases old KRAs → reinforces wrong behaviour.
  5. Bosses cut HRD (no visible return).
  6. Good people leave.
  7. Strategy itself fails (quality/digital/expansion).
  8. Activity metrics hide failure until crisis.

Q37. Two benefits: career planning + potential integrated

  1. B1: REALISTIC paths — less false hope/betrayal; honest counselling.
  2. B2: USE talent in time — stretch, succession, money on the right people.
  3. Eg: two equal engineers → leader vs designer paths; both stay.
  4. Also fewer wrong promotions.
  5. Also retain & motivate.
  6. Also better counselling (evidence not ‘work hard and rise’).
  7. Match/mismatch step in career process NEEDS potential data.
  8. Labels without moves = bitterness.

Q38. Two features of effective HRD practice

  1. F1: developmental PURPOSE in reality (IDP/coaching happens after).
  2. Test: did work behaviour change? Help or hinder process?
  3. F2: INTEGRATION with other subsystems + strategy.
  4. Eg: quality course → next appraisal item + boss coaches.
  5. Also: need-based, line-owned, fair, funded, climate-safe, evaluated.
  6. Fair access (not only favourites).
  7. Features = what you OBSERVE, not brochure.
  8. Two long + cluster = 8 marks.

Q39. Design integrated system (6 elements connected)

  1. One policy: line owns, HRD facilitates, committee 2×/year.
  2. One competency language + one employee file (spine).
  3. Cycle: goals → ongoing feedback → appraisal → counselling/IDP → potential → career → training/OJT → evaluate.
  4. Quarterly check-ins; trained listeners; confidentiality.
  5. IDPs roll into calendar; TNA still validates group courses; boss transfer brief.
  6. Talent council: potential → rotation; specialist path exists; talk TO employee.
  7. Climate so data isn’t fake.
  8. Yearly audit of LINKS (did IDP happen? did high-pots get stretch?).

Q40. Evaluate strong training content, weak strategy/appraisal/career links

  1. Keep: good teaching may give high L1–L2.
  2. Cost: may teach WRONG capability vs strategy.
  3. No appraisal hook → no transfer (L3 dies).
  4. No career credit → only self-driven apply.
  5. Likely L1 high, L3/L4 poor; smile sheets mislead.
  6. ‘Holiday training’ cynicism.
  7. May help a few individuals — not enough as org HRD.
  8. Verdict: DON’T accept as-is. Remap to TNA/strategy, pre-post goals, career map, then re-evaluate.
UNIT 3 — Q41–60

Q41. TNA concept + importance

  1. Stage 1 of cycle: TNA → design → delivery → evaluate.
  2. Need ≈ standard − actual ONLY if cause is KSA (not broken machine).
  3. Org analysis: goals, where it hurts, will climate support?
  4. Task analysis: important tasks + KSAs (content skeleton).
  5. Person analysis: WHO has the gap; ‘knows but doesn’t do’ ≠ training.
  6. Importance: stop off-the-shelf waste; honest diagnosis.
  7. Importance: design quality, fairness, priority.
  8. Importance: baseline for Kirkpatrick. Skip TNA = other 3 stages guess.

Q42. Steps in designing a training programme

  1. Confirm TNA, trainees, readiness (is training still the answer?).
  2. Write goal + observable objectives (‘able to do X in Y minutes’).
  3. Content + sequence (concept → demo → practice).
  4. Methods: OJT / off-job / e-learning; internal vs vendor; site.
  5. Facilities, trainer, materials, budget (notes’ matrix).
  6. Design TRANSFER: boss brief, action plan, job aid.
  7. Build evaluation now (tests, 30–60 day behaviour, results vs baseline).
  8. Check feasibility + equity (can people attend? women/branches included?).

Q43. On-the-job vs off-the-job methods + examples

  1. OJT = learn while doing real work with a guide.
  2. OJT eg: coaching, rotation, apprentice, understudy, JIT, live project.
  3. OJT + : relevant, cheaper, transfer easy. − : bad coach, bad habits, danger.
  4. Off-job = away from workstation (class/vestibule/institute).
  5. Off-job eg: lecture, case, role play, games, simulation, vestibule, courses.
  6. Off-job + : focus, expert, safe, concepts. − : cost, transfer gap, irrelevance.
  7. Choose from objective/safety, not ‘which is best forever’.
  8. HRD blend: concept off-job → skill OJT.

Q44. E-learning + advantages

  1. Def: LMS/modules/webinar/mobile; self-paced or live; often blended. PDF dump ≠ e-learning.
  2. Adv: anytime/anywhere (shifts, many cities).
  3. Adv: scale + same message fast (compliance/product update).
  4. Adv: low extra cost after design; easy repeats.
  5. Adv: standard quality + tracking scores.
  6. Adv: replay, micro-learning, some personalisation.
  7. Adv: blend with wiki/webinar/social learning (21st-c notes).
  8. Limits: net/device, dropouts, weak for counselling skill, still need boss for transfer.

Q45. Kirkpatrick model

  1. L1 Reaction: like it? smile sheet. Useful but weak alone.
  2. L2 Learning: KSA up? test/demo/pre-post.
  3. L3 Behaviour: use on job after weeks? observe/boss/audit. Climate decides.
  4. L4 Results: quality, errors, safety, CSAT, cost — corporate objectives.
  5. Ladder: each can fail after the previous succeeded.
  6. Plan measures at TNA; take baseline.
  7. Use: diagnose (high L1 low L3 → transfer not trainer).
  8. Limit: L4 many causes; costly; use deeper for high-risk training.

Q46. Importance of effective DELIVERY after design

  1. Notes: implementation = admin arrangements + carrying out training.
  2. Learning happens in the room, not in the file.
  3. Admin (hall, login, batch size) = respect + attention.
  4. Trainer skill (adult learning, not slide-reading).
  5. Adapt pace; psychological safety to practise/fail.
  6. Start transfer IN class: real cases, action plan, boss at close.
  7. Open/close = why it matters Monday.
  8. Collect tests/reaction here or evaluation dies.

Q47. Relationship TNA–design–delivery–evaluation

  1. Cycle: TNA → design → delivery → evaluation → back to TNA.
  2. TNA feeds objectives/content/method.
  3. Design is the script delivery performs.
  4. Delivery produces the evidence evaluation measures.
  5. Evaluation improves next TNA and redesigns method/admin/climate.
  6. Skip TNA → excellent class on WRONG need.
  7. Skip evaluation → open loop, no professional HRD.
  8. Budget all four, not only the visible class day.

Q48. Importance of evaluating training (employee + org)

  1. Employee: know what they mastered (feedback).
  2. Employee: career/employability proof.
  3. Employee: voice to improve next programme.
  4. Org: accountability for money + lost production.
  5. Org: find the break (need/design/delivery/transfer).
  6. Org: prove strategy/capability (L4).
  7. Avoid illusions: happy≠able; strict≠failed.
  8. HRD credibility → future resources for people.

Q49. Repeated errors: what info to collect (apply TNA)

  1. Error facts: type, frequency, step, shift, cost (records+observe).
  2. Org: goals, new machine/target, speed vs quality rewards.
  3. NON-training causes first: machine, SOP, staff shortage, incentives.
  4. Task: correct procedure, critical KSAs, where errors sit.
  5. Person: who, new vs old, knows-but-doesn’t-do vs doesn’t-know.
  6. Readiness/fear of reporting.
  7. Will boss allow slower correct method after class?
  8. Baseline error rate now = later evaluation. Train ONLY if KSA gap.

Q50. Methods for BOTH practical skill + conceptual knowledge

  1. Split objectives: explain WHY vs DO the task.
  2. Concepts: short lecture, diagram, case, e-module, quiz.
  3. Safe practice: simulation, vestibule, role play, dummy.
  4. Then OJT + trained coach + checklist.
  5. Action-learning live project binds head+hand.
  6. Micro e-learning to replay concepts later.
  7. Sequence: concept → demo → simulate → OJT → review. Don’t start unsupervised OJT.
  8. Evaluate BOTH: knowledge test + observed job sample.

Q51. Any two purposes of TNA

  1. P1: find genuine KSA gaps (who/what) + example.
  2. P2: stop false training when cause is system/tool/pay (notes’ caveat).
  3. Also set measurable objectives for design.
  4. Also pick right trainees & methods.
  5. Also prioritise scarce money.
  6. Also baseline for evaluation.
  7. Also link to strategy (org analysis).
  8. Two long paras + cluster.

Q52. Name any two Kirkpatrick levels

  1. Name L1 Reaction + how measured + limit.
  2. Name L2 Learning + test/demo.
  3. Briefly L3 Behaviour (job after lag).
  4. Briefly L4 Results (org outcomes).
  5. Example measures each.
  6. Ladder: one doesn’t prove the next.
  7. Costly/safety programmes need more than L1.
  8. Two named in depth + rest brief = 8 marks.

Q53. Why high reaction but no job-performance gain

  1. Entertainment ≠ learning (celebrity/hill station).
  2. Too easy / only theory — happy and still incompetent.
  3. Wrong need (TNA missing) — training can’t fix machine/pay.
  4. Boss: ‘forget class’; peers mock new method.
  5. No chance to use skill for months → forgotten.
  6. Rewards contradict (taught quality, paid speed).
  7. No follow-up/coaching/IDP — one picnic.
  8. L1 weak predictor of L3/L4. Design for transfer not applause.

Q54. Consequences of design without TNA

  1. Wrong content → original errors continue.
  2. Wrong audience (bored experts, missing needy).
  3. Wrong method (lecture where OJT needed).
  4. False solution: mgmt stops looking, blames workers next time.
  5. Cynicism; next HRD ignored.
  6. Money + lost production wasted.
  7. No baseline → evaluation is theatre.
  8. Calendar ritual → strategic capability lag till crisis.

Q55. Evaluate usefulness of Kirkpatrick

  1. Useful: complete vs smile sheets; matches notes (learning/behaviour/impact).
  2. Useful: language with managers; plan measures at design.
  3. Useful: diagnose which link broke.
  4. Limit: L4 many causes — don’t over-claim profit.
  5. Limit: costly follow-up; firms stop at L1 (model unused).
  6. Limit: not every 1-hour talk needs full L4 — proportionate.
  7. Use well: baseline, transfer design, modest L4.
  8. Verdict: best simple GUIDE, not a magic proof. Keep it.

Q56. How method choice (OJT / off-job / e-learn) affects outcomes

  1. OJT: good transfer if coach skilled; bad coach = mass wrong habits; danger.
  2. Off-job: better concepts/safe practice; transfer dies if work unlike class.
  3. E-learn: scale, tracking, knowledge; dropouts; weak people-skills alone.
  4. FIT to objective beats fashion (motor vs info vs interpersonal).
  5. Learner/context: new vs old; many cities → e-learn.
  6. BLEND often best (covers each weakness).
  7. Climate can kill ALL methods at L3.
  8. Outcomes = fit + execution + transfer, not the method’s brand name.

Q57. Two reasons to evaluate at MORE than one level

  1. R1: each level = different question (like ≠ learn ≠ do ≠ results).
  2. Eg fire course popular but can’t use extinguisher.
  3. R2: diagnosis — know whether to fix content, trainer, or workplace.
  4. Also accountability for money/time.
  5. Avoid false success (high smiles) and false failure (strict but learned).
  6. Gives learner further coaching data.
  7. Strategic HRD cannot live on L1.
  8. Two long + extras.

Q58. Two factors in selecting a training method

  1. F1: learning objectives (K / S / A need different methods) + example.
  2. F2: nature of task (danger → simulate first; workplace teaches wrong → off-job first).
  3. Also trainees (language, literacy, numbers).
  4. Also cost/time/production loss.
  5. Also location/facilities/trainer available.
  6. Also urgency of transfer.
  7. Also culture (will role-play be accepted?).
  8. Systematic from TNA, not habit.

Q59. Design complete programme (one skill gap, full cycle)

  1. Pick gap e.g. complaint-handling (high repeat complaints).
  2. TNA: org/task/person + non-training checks + baseline CSAT/repeat %.
  3. Objectives: role-play 80% checklist; 8-week job metric drop.
  4. Method blend: e-policy + class role play + 2-week OJT coach.
  5. Delivery: batch ~12, working CRM, manager at close, action plan.
  6. Transfer: weekly coach, job aid, appraisal KRA on quality.
  7. Eval L1–2: form, test, observed role play.
  8. Eval L3–4: 30/60-day audits vs baseline; 60-day review meeting.

Q60. Evaluate two programmes with Kirkpatrick + recommend

  1. Need eg: supervisors must give developmental feedback.
  2. A: 2-day off-site celebrity ‘leadership’, no practice/follow-up.
  3. B: TNA, role play, job assignments, boss-as-coach, 60-day follow-up, appraisal item.
  4. L1: A often wins (venue). Don’t decide on L1.
  5. L2: B wins (practised skill).
  6. L3: B wins (workplace support); A rarely transfers.
  7. L4: B likelier climate/error/engagement gain; A costly flop.
  8. Recommend B + condition: top support for coaching time. Popularity ≠ effectiveness.
